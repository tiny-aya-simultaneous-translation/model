# v0.3 recipe re-validation report — text+audio, full corpus (`v03-5k-reval-ta`)

> Public artifact: shareable as-is. Condensed version on the
> [v0.3 model card](hf-model-card-tr-hi-s2st-v0.3.md); full engineering trail on
> [PR #10](https://github.com/tiny-aya-simultaneous-translation/model/pull/10).
> Date: 2026-07-09.

## Headline

Six hyperparameter arms re-validated the v0.3 recipe as a **text+audio** speech-to-speech
translator on the full **1.24 M-pair** synthetic TR↔HI corpus — 5,000 steps (≈1.03 epoch)
per arm at global batch 256, one single-host TPU v6e-8 per arm. Winner: **arm D
(`lora_exclude_top: 0` — LoRA adapters on all 36 backbone layers)**. The previously frozen
champion recipe (arm A) finished **last at every composite weighting**: the re-validation
was not a formality.

Two context notes that make this run meaningful:
- v0.1–v0.2 trained **audio-only** on the false belief that the corpus shipped no text
  alignments; that was a two-bug misdiagnosis (wrong filenames in the check, wrong
  directory in the loader), corrected 2026-07-08 — the corpus has **100% alignment
  coverage** (840,426 src + 840,426 tgt word-level files). This sweep is the first
  supervised-text training in the project's history.
- Before this sweep's winner goes to production, the full shipping stack passed a
  **pipeline-validation gate** (32-sample memorization + checkpoint-reload + teacher-forced
  AND autoregressive inference examination) — see below.

Base recipe all arms share: r=32 (unless stated), α=2r, rsLoRA, +MLP target modules,
lr_lora 1.716e-4 (unless stated), `text_weight 0.2` (constant), dropout .05 / wd .01
(unless stated), global batch 256, bf16, scan recipe.

**Selection notes:**
- The composite weighting question was resolved **analytically**: since the composite is
  a metric (not a training weight), the ranking was recomputed post-hoc from the logged
  `val/{text,audio}_loss` series at three weightings — it is **unanimous**
  (E ≺ D ≺ C ≺ F ≺ B ≺ A at 0.2/0.8, 0.4/0.6 and 0.5/0.5), so the choice of weighting
  does not affect the conclusion.
- **Gate adjudication** (pre-registered): cb0 accuracy must sit within ~0.5 pt of the best
  arm. E (−1.1 pt) and C (−1.2 pt) fail it; **D is the gates-clean winner** (−0.3 pt,
  second-best at everything else). Honest note: E's composite lead over D is 0.015
  (~0.4%); the **D+E combination** (exclude_top=0 *plus* the stronger regularization) was
  never tested and is the obvious future probe.
- All six arms pass the text gate by an order of magnitude (worst text CE 1.69 vs the
  11.5 stuck-at-random threshold).
- Arm F (unfreezing 2 frozen Moshi depth blocks) bought +0.06 pt mean CB1–7 over A at
  ~20% step-time cost — not carried into production.
- Key scientific read: **top-layer adapters are the text lever.** Removing the top-2
  layer exclusion (D) or adding regularization (E) both improve text CE by ~0.5 over the
  frozen champion at zero audio cost; rank (B: r64) and depth-unfreezing (F) do not.

## Pipeline-validation gate (precondition for production — PASSED)

Full report on [PR #10](https://github.com/tiny-aya-simultaneous-translation/model/pull/10);
32 fixed examples memorized on the exact shipping stack, then examined through an
independent checkpoint reload:

- **CB0 teacher-forced accuracy 99.6%** (train-val) / 99.5% (independent reload+eval);
  CB1–7 = 97.9→88.6% — the frozen-depth-decoder ceiling, at parity with the pre-scan
  stack (no regression from the XLA changes).
- **Text 99.8%**, decoded predictions **character-identical** to targets in both
  directions; all per-component losses → ~0.
- **Greedy AR reproduction: CB0 100.0%**, all-CB match numerically identical to the TF
  accuracy — after the gate **caught a real off-by-one in the AR evaluation loop**
  (predictions shifted one frame + conditioned on a placeholder). Disclosure: AR/ASR-BLEU
  numbers reported for earlier versions used the broken decoding and understate quality.

## Method (reproducibility)

- One arm per single-host v6e-8 (TRC spot, europe-west4-a); deterministic configs
  generated by `scripts/tpu/launch_reval_arms.sh` (arm matrix = single source of truth);
  no W&B agent — plain grid with `--resume auto`.
- Cost per arm: 5,000 steps × ~9.3 s/step ≈ **13 h** on 8 × Trillium chips; peaks
  25.7–29.4 GiB of 31.25 GiB HBM/chip (r64 arm the tightest).
- Data: `tiny-aya-translate/tr-hi-mimi-encoded`, 1,178,302 train / 62,036 val after the
  ~5% missing-`.pt` filter; word-level alignments at the dataset root (see the loader
  note on the model card — the split manifests point at legacy names).
- Infra prerequisites (all documented in `docs/tpu-runbook.md`): the v6e-8 scan recipe
  (9 blockers fixed — PR #10), `micro_mark_step`, FlexibleLinear bmm patch, jax/torchax/
  flax pins.

# Appendix: pre-production optimization probes (2026-07-10)

Before launch, two team suggestions (raise `clip_grad_norm` 1.0→10, larger global batch)
were probed on idle v6e-8s against arm D's own trajectory.

**CORRECTION (2026-07-10, same day): the probes did NOT test clip 10.** A config-key
audit found `train.clip_grad_norm` was a **dead key** — the trainer read only
`train.max_grad_norm`, whose default of 1.0 silently won everywhere. Both probe configs
set `clip_grad_norm: 10.0`, so **both probes actually ran at clip 1.0**. Every sweep arm
and the production config *intend* clip 1.0 (= the default), so no trained run behaved
differently than its logs show; only the probe interpretation changes. The key is now
honored (normalized onto `max_grad_norm` in `load_config`, regression-tested, and the
startup banner prints the effective clip).

What the probes *actually* measured (both at clip 1.0):

| samples seen | D (clip1, 256) | P1 = D rerun, shorter cosine ([8jchq4yu](https://wandb.ai/cataluna84/tinyaya-stage2-tpu/runs/8jchq4yu)) | P3 = batch 512 @ clip 1 ([x198ay7a](https://wandb.ai/cataluna84/tinyaya-stage2-tpu/runs/x198ay7a)) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 128k | 4.434 | 4.324 (−2.5%) | 4.259 (−3.9%) |
| 256k | 4.079 | 3.918 (−3.9%) | 3.938 (−3.5%) |
| **384k (decision)** | **3.842** | 3.865 (+0.6%) | 3.885 (+1.1%) |

- **P1** became arm D's exact config with only `max_steps` 5000→1500 — a cosine-schedule
  ablation. Its early lead / late deficit is the schedule (faster lr decay), and its
  final **+0.6% despite an end-of-schedule advantage bounds run-to-run noise at ≈0.6%**,
  right under the 1% decision bar — useful calibration for all future probe deltas.
- **P3** inadvertently ran the probe we had mooted: **global batch 512 at clip 1.0**. It
  lost (+1.1% worse, cb0 −6pt) *with* the schedule confound in its favor, so the
  batch-512 rejection stands on its own.

**Verdict unchanged (pre-registered rule: >1% win at equal samples or keep validated
settings): production runs clip 1.0 @ batch 256.** Clip 10 is now **untested** rather
than rejected; testing it for real is optional and would ride a future user-authorized
slice (grad norms 1.5–9.5 all clip to 1.0, so the lr was calibrated under normalized
gradients — changing that remains a regime change requiring evidence). W&B group:
`v03-prod-probes`.
